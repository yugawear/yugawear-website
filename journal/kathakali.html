What is Kathakali?

Kathakali is a highly stylized dance-drama tradition from Kerala, India. It combines acting, dance, music, expressive facial movements, hand gestures and elaborate visual design to tell stories.

Its most recognizable qualities are its dramatic makeup, monumental costumes, expressive eyes, codified gestures and powerful movements. Together, these elements turn the performer into a visual storyteller.

Kathakali does not simply tell a story. It turns the story into an image.

A performer enters the stage carrying an entire visual vocabulary: painted skin, an elaborate headpiece, layered costume, controlled movement and eyes capable of expressing an emotion without a spoken sentence.

In Kathakali, colour identifies character, gestures communicate meaning, facial expressions carry emotion and music creates the dramatic atmosphere. The result is one of India's most distinctive traditions of performance.

01

A performance shaped by many traditions.

The history of Kathakali can be traced to the 17th century. Its development emerged from earlier theatrical, ritual, folk and martial traditions of Kerala.

Kerala Tourism identifies influences from traditions such as Kutiyattam, Krishnanattam, Ashtapadiyattam, Mudiyettu, Kalamezhuthu and Theyyam, as well as the martial art Kalaripayattu.

Kathakali also developed through the tradition of dance-drama and narrative performance. Its stories frequently draw upon the Ramayana, Mahabharata and Puranic traditions.

02

The body becomes a visual language.

Kathakali is built around the idea that the body can communicate without ordinary speech.

The performer uses controlled movements, facial expressions, eye movements and hand gestures to communicate character, emotion and narrative.

This creates a highly codified performance language. A gesture is not merely decorative. It can carry meaning within the story.

When movement becomes language, the smallest gesture can carry an entire story.

This visual communication is one of the reasons Kathakali feels so cinematic even when experienced on a traditional stage.

03

Colour becomes character.

Few elements define Kathakali more strongly than its makeup.

The facial makeup, known as vesham, does more than beautify the performer. It establishes character type and communicates qualities through colour, pattern and form.

Major makeup categories include Pacha, Kathi, Thaadi, Kari and Minukku. Pacha, dominated by green, is generally associated with noble or heroic characters, while other categories communicate different character qualities.

The white Chutti surrounding the jawline further enlarges the face and separates the character from ordinary human appearance.

04

Expression, gesture & movement.

Kathakali's storytelling depends heavily on physical expression.

The eyes, eyebrows, facial muscles, hands and body are trained to work together. Performers use mudras, stylized gestures and controlled movements to communicate ideas and emotions.

Facial expression is especially important. Kathakali uses the tradition of navarasa — nine emotional states — as part of its expressive vocabulary.

The relationship between movement and emotion is what allows a performer to shift between characters, moods and dramatic situations without changing the basic stage environment.

05

Story is carried by sound.

Kathakali is not a silent visual performance. Music and percussion create the rhythm and dramatic structure around the performer.

Traditional performances can feature instruments including the chenda, maddalam, chengila, ilathaalam and idakka.

Singers narrate and sing the verses while the performer interprets the story through movement, gesture and expression.

The result is a carefully coordinated relationship between visual performance and sound: the music creates atmosphere, while the performer translates the narrative into physical form.

Why does Kathakali look so powerful?

Its visual power comes from exaggeration and control.

The large circular headgear, layered costumes, white Chutti, dramatic makeup and broad silhouettes make the performer visible from a distance.

At the same time, the performance depends on extremely precise details: the direction of the eyes, the position of the hands, facial muscles and timing of movement.

This creates an interesting visual contrast. Kathakali is monumental from far away, yet extraordinarily detailed up close.

07

From performance to cultural identity.

Kathakali has become one of the most recognizable visual symbols of Kerala's performing arts.

Its distinctive combination of colour, costume, makeup, movement and storytelling has allowed the tradition to communicate across language barriers and cultural contexts.

The art form continues to be performed, taught and developed through institutions and artists dedicated to preserving Kerala's performing traditions.

Its importance lies not simply in preserving an old performance style, but in maintaining a sophisticated visual system in which identity, narrative and emotion are communicated through the body.

Kathakali FAQ.

What is Kathakali?

Kathakali is a highly stylized dance-drama tradition from Kerala. It combines acting, dance, music, expressive facial movements, hand gestures and elaborate visual design.

When did Kathakali originate?

The history of Kathakali can be traced to the 17th century. Its development was influenced by earlier theatrical, ritual, folk and martial traditions of Kerala.

What do the colours in Kathakali makeup mean?

Kathakali makeup uses different colours and patterns to communicate character qualities. Green Pacha makeup, for example, is associated with noble and heroic qualities.

What stories are performed in Kathakali?

Kathakali performances commonly draw their narratives from Indian epics and mythology, including stories from the Ramayana, Mahabharata and Puranas.

What makes Kathakali visually distinctive?

Kathakali combines elaborate makeup, monumental costumes, headgear, mudras, facial expressions, stylized movement and dramatic music to create its distinctive visual language.
