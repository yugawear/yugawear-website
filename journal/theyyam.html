THEY YAM: RITUAL, COLOUR & IDENTITY.

Where ritual, colour, movement and mythology transform performance into a living cultural identity.

What is Theyyam?

Theyyam is a ritual art tradition of North Malabar in Kerala that combines dance, mime, music, elaborate costumes, facial makeup and ritual performance.

More than a staged performance, Theyyam exists within a ritual environment. Performances take place primarily at sacred groves, shrines and traditional family spaces, where the performer, known as the Kolam, becomes the centre of the ritual experience.

Theyyam is not simply performed. It is experienced as a ritual.

In the northern districts of Kerala, colour, sound, movement and mythology come together in a form that has become one of the strongest visual identities of North Malabar.

A performer enters with an enormous headpiece, an intensely painted face, layered ornaments and a costume built specifically for the character being represented. Around them, drums, songs, ritual spaces and spectators become part of the experience.

01

A tradition rooted in North Malabar.

Theyyam is predominantly associated with North Malabar, particularly the present-day districts of Kannur and Kasaragod, with traditions also found in parts of Wayanad and northern Kozhikode.

Kerala Tourism describes Theyyam as a socio-religious ritual that has existed in the region for a very long period. The tradition incorporates elements of dance, mime, music, mythology and local cultural practices.

Its stories are connected not only with divine figures but also with local heroes, ancestors, spirits, animals, trees and regional legends. This relationship with local history makes every form of Theyyam deeply connected to its place.

NORTH MALABAR Kannur and Kasaragod are among the principal regions associated with the Theyyam tradition.
02

Performance becomes ritual.

Unlike a conventional stage performance, Theyyam is traditionally performed within sacred and community spaces.

Performances can take place at kaavu, sacred groves, village shrines and traditional family houses known as tharavadu.

The performer undergoes a period of preparation and discipline before the ritual. Kerala Tourism describes this preparation as Vrutham, involving fasting, prayer, meditation and a disciplined lifestyle.

The stage disappears. The ritual space becomes the world of the performance.

The opening invocation, known as thottam, establishes the ritual context before the main performance develops.

03

Colour becomes identity.

The first thing many people notice about Theyyam is its extraordinary colour.

Red, orange, yellow, black and white appear across the face, body, costumes and ornaments. These colours are not simply decorative. They form part of the visual identity of individual Theyyam characters.

Traditional face and body painting uses natural materials. Kerala Tourism describes techniques such as Parunthuvaal Ezhuthu and Anchupulli Ezhuthu for body painting, alongside several traditional face painting methods.

Red is particularly prominent in Theyyam makeup and is traditionally produced using materials including turmeric and limestone.

04

The body becomes another form.

The costume of Theyyam is not a single standard outfit. Each form has its own visual vocabulary.

The most recognisable element is the mudi — the elaborate headgear whose size, construction and form can dramatically change the silhouette of the performer.

Costumes can incorporate coconut fronds, areca leaves, bamboo, shells, ornaments and other locally available materials. Some forms use highly elaborate structures, while others are comparatively restrained.

Face masks, breastplates, ornaments, bangles and anklets further transform the performer. Every component contributes to the identity of the particular Theyyam being represented.

05

Rhythm creates the transformation.

Sound is fundamental to the Theyyam experience.

Traditional instruments such as the chenda, elathalam, drums, conches and utukku accompany performances, while songs known as thottam contribute to the narrative and ritual atmosphere.

The performer responds to the rhythm through movement, creating an increasingly intense relationship between sound, costume, body and ritual space.

The result is not simply a dance accompanied by music. It is a complete sensory environment in which sound, movement and visual form operate together.

06

More than art. A living identity.

Theyyam occupies a distinctive position within the cultural life of North Malabar.

Many Theyyam stories are connected with local heroes, heroines, ancestors, deities and regional legends. Some performances preserve memories of people and events that have become part of local cultural history.

Kerala Tourism notes that the tradition includes hundreds of different forms, each with its own mythology, visual language, costume and performance practice.

This makes Theyyam more than a collection of costumes or dance movements. It is a cultural system in which place, community, belief, memory and visual identity remain closely connected.

RITUAL → MEMORY → IDENTITY Theyyam connects performance with the cultural memory of the communities that sustain it.
07

Why does Theyyam feel so powerful?

Its visual power comes from contrast.

A human performer becomes visually monumental through the combination of an enlarged silhouette, intense colour, geometric face painting, elaborate ornaments and dramatic headgear.

At the same time, the materials remain closely connected to the region: coconut leaves, bamboo, areca materials, natural pigments and locally crafted ornaments.

The result is a visual language that feels both ancient and immediate — highly symbolic, highly physical and deeply connected to place.

Theyyam FAQ.

What is Theyyam?

Theyyam is a ritual art tradition of North Malabar in Kerala combining dance, mime, music, elaborate costumes, facial makeup and ritual performance.

Where is Theyyam performed?

Theyyam is predominantly performed in North Malabar, especially in the Kannur and Kasaragod regions, with traditions also found in parts of Wayanad and northern Kozhikode.

What does Theyyam mean?

Theyyam is commonly associated with the Malayalam word Daivam, meaning God or divine being.

How many types of Theyyam are there?

Kerala Tourism describes around 400 different Theyyam forms, including major and minor forms with distinctive stories, costumes and rituals.

Why is Theyyam famous for its colours and costumes?

Each Theyyam form can have its own elaborate headgear, costume, ornaments and facial makeup. Red, orange, yellow, black and white are among the prominent colours used in its visual language.
